## Hypotheses and conclusions retained

- The [analytic regularity theorem](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/GroundTheory/AutomorphicForms/PartialDifferential/AnalyticHypoelliptic.lean#L336-L341) allows complex coefficients analytic in **real** variables. Ellipticity is pointwise on the nonzero real cotangent directions. The short proof includes order zero and dimension zero.
- The [integration theorem](https://github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/GroundTheory/AutomorphicForms/PartialDifferential/EllipticParametrix.lean#L119-L130) assumes smooth coefficients, positive dimension and order, and $n<2m$. It allows every open $U$ containing $x_0$ and contained in the coefficient domain. The smaller ball in the proof, a positive lower symbol bound there, and the Sobolev exponent $n/2<s<m$ are constructed from these hypotheses.
- $E$ is an integrable function with compact topological support inside $U$ and a smooth representative off $x_0$. The other function $\beta$ is globally smooth with compact topological support inside the same $U$.
- The test function only needs to be smooth on $U$. One cutoff equal to one near both supports and $x_0$ extends the distributional identity to that class of test functions. The transpose acts in the complex bilinear pairing, with no complex conjugation.
- The exponential chart, analytic expression for the Laplacian, and its smooth positive Haar density are inputs supplied in the consuming group-theoretic argument. The kernel adjusts to the normalization of the given Haar measure.

**Variable-coefficient check.** On $(-1,1)$ take $a(x)=1+ix$ and $P\phi=(a\phi)'$, an elliptic operator with a nonzero zeroth-order term. Its complex bilinear transpose is $Tv=-a v'$. At $x_0=0$ take $H$ to be the Heaviside function and $\eta$ a smooth cutoff compactly supported in $(-1,1)$ and equal to one near zero. Then $E=-\eta H$ and $\beta=-a\eta'H$ give

$$\int E\,P\phi=-\int_0^1\eta(a\phi)'=\phi(0)+\int_0^1\eta'a\phi=\phi(0)-\int\beta\phi.$$

All terms are determined by the restriction of $\phi$ to $(-1,1)$. The jump of $E$ is allowed, and $\beta$ is smooth even at zero because $\eta'$ vanishes there.

**Classical inputs.** For Proposition 1.1 the characteristic set on the nonzero real cotangent vectors is empty, so analytic microlocal regularity makes the analytic wavefront set empty. In Proposition 1.2 the scalar transpose is elliptic on a ball compactly contained in the given neighbourhood. Grubb's Corollary 7.19 supplies the right parametrix there. The point mass belongs to $H^{-s}$ for $s>n/2$, and $n<2m$ permits $s<m$. The local Sobolev mapping estimate gives a locally square-integrable representative. The two cutoffs in the proof provide compact support and allow test functions smooth only on the prescribed open set. [Hörmander, Chapter VIII](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-61497-2) · [Grubb, Corollaries 7.19 and 7.20](https://web.math.ku.dk/~grubb/dist7n.pdf)

**Order of choices.** The group input supplies one chart operator for every translated test function. Fix the positive-degree monic polynomial, the identity neighbourhood, and the Haar measure before constructing the kernels. The inverse formula uses negative exponential coordinates for the function and the Haar density in the positive coordinates for the integral. In both conventions the kernel works for every smooth null solution and every translate. Including the zero polynomial would make every smooth function a null solution. On the additive group $\mathbb R$ this would require a smooth density to represent the point mass at zero.
