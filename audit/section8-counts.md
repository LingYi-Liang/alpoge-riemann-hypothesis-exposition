## The high-conductor argument

Write $N=f_E$, $b=c_2(\xi)$ and $R_1=16o(\ell M)^2 4^{n_0}$. The source's statement called Count V assumes both $\ell\in X_\psi,\ r=1$ and $R_1\le2^b$. In its proof the first pair of conditions is unused. The entire conjunction is supplied as the variable `hP`, whose only use after introduction is `hP.2` in the call to `hostData_of_host`. That declaration accepts any odd $\ell$, any $r\ge1$, an admitted host character of the required parity, and the displayed threshold. The same original automorphic and cohomological inputs are supplied before the counts at the window call. [Exact count predicates](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/HeightAndVanishing/Counts.lean#L60-L73) · [Host data from the threshold](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/UnitaryThreeDeformations/HostDataOf.lean#L9-L23) · [Proof of V](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/CountVOf.lean#L33-L60) · [Discharge of the Gaussian inputs](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/LinkedUnlE.lean#L55-L61)

Fix the Gaussian field before any host, prime, twist or coefficient ring. Its counting theorem then supplies one exponent. The frame and the reduced unitary lift supply a finite trace family and a natural number $v$ bounding the unnormalized length. If $s$ is the sum of its normalized trace-difference valuations, the two source inequalities are $v\le2e_{\mathcal O}s$ and $s\log\ell\le(\ell N r)^C$. They give finite length and the required bound on the log-weighted normalized length. This reconstruction uses the proof of the Gaussian estimate underneath Count V. The abstract implication in the published statement of Count V alone does not justify dropping either hypothesis. The sum also has the required uniformity. A high term forces $\ell^2\le R_1\le2^b\le N$. For any finite prime set there are at most $N$ active primes and at most $N$ host characters per prime and twist. Summing over $1\le r<t$ gives at most $(N^2t)^{C+2}$. For the remaining low terms of I, apply the source's two-test-prime bound to the finite set of primes with a relevant low term in each kernel orbit. Nonnegativity bounds this restricted sum by the full orbit sum. The old test primes and the order in which they were chosen remain valid. [Orbit sum](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/HeightAndVanishing/Counts.lean#L24-L31) · [Downstream finite-set and pointwise requirements](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/HeightAndVanishing/Counts.lean#L53-L63)

For a concrete overlap, take the trivial base character. Here $M=o=1$, $p_*=7$, $h_*=1$, $p_1=3$ and $X_\psi=\{3,7\}$. At a sufficiently high real dyadic layer choose an even character of conductor $2^{17}$ and order $2^{15}$, trivial on the odd components. At $\ell=11$ it has $a=1$, $f_0=2$, $n_0=3$, and $123904=R_1<2^{17}$. For $t>10$, the same character at $r=2$ belongs to I, and at $r=10$ to II. Both use the Gaussian proof. In contrast, the product of the quadratic characters at the two auxiliary primes is a nontrivial even quadratic character with $2^b=1$. At the same $\ell,r=10$ it still requires the low-conductor estimate.

## The common symplectic level

On the original large-host range, nonexceptional resonance implies $\ell\ge5$, $r\ge2$ and $\xi_P=\xi$. Thus outside $X_\psi$ all nontrivial resonant characters belong to II or III. Inside $X_\psi$ their union consists precisely of $r\ge2$ with $\operatorname{ord}(\xi_P)$ even. The auxiliary character remains trivial at level one in II and has exact order $\operatorname{ord}(\xi)$ at $p_1$ in III. [Host case split](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/HeightAndVanishing/Cases.lean#L8-L46) · [Native character data](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/AutomorphicCases/SymplecticGSp4.lean#L12-L26) · [Construction in III](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/SymplecticGSp4/DataEx.lean#L414-L439)

The previous numerical estimate used $2^{n_0}<\ell^{\varphi(4o)}$. The valuation calculation in Lemma 8.A instead gives $2^{n_0}\le K(\ell+1)$ with $K=2^{v_2(\varphi(4o))}$. The old inequality itself follows from $2^{n_0}\le\ell^{f_0}-1$ and $f_0\mid\varphi(4o)$. For instance, one may take $D$ to be the least power of two at least $(64oM^2+4K^2Mp_1+8)N\ell^3$. For a given coefficient field its extension by $\mu_D$ serves every character and twist of this host at $\ell$. Apply the same bound directly to the extended character. This avoids separate transport identities for the two auxiliary depths in the enlargement step. The native automorphic data and coefficient-independence theorem are retained. ## Recovery of the source outputs

| Original case | What the revised argument delivers |
|---|---|
| I | The high part has a polynomial sum even before imposing I. Low terms keep the original orbit integer and the two fixed test primes. Their sum gives the original bound on every finite prime set. |
| II | A finite length and a polynomial pointwise bound. The Gaussian proof applies throughout the high part. The low part uses the common symplectic estimate. |
| III | The same finite length and pointwise bound with the original auxiliary character at $p_1$. An odd-order character has trivial dyadic component, so this case is covered by the symplectic part. |
| IV | The original field-at-a-fixed-layer comparison and its sum over twists, including the layer term, are retained. |
| V | The original bound at $r=1$, with equality in the threshold on the Gaussian side. Below it, $[L:\mathbb Q]\le\ell\operatorname{ord}\xi\ll_\psi\ell^5$ and $\ell$ belongs to the fixed exceptional set. This suffices in the original duality and fixed-degree estimate. |
| Z | Exact vanishing of the trivial-character group from the source, including the cases where the mod-$\ell$ cyclotomic twist is quadratic. |

The dyadic prime, the choice of the pair, the torsion window and the comparison of the two twist cutoffs keep their original proofs. ## Verification and length

The [Lean certificate](Section8Counts.lean) checks the case predicates, the dyadic bounds and common levels, the numerical rank-four count, the Gaussian length normalization, and summation on bounded prime support. It compiles with Lean 4.33.1 at Mathlib revision `0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474`. Its eight named results were replayed with `leanchecker --verbose Section8Counts` in the imported Mathlib environment. The extension of the Gaussian arithmetic argument was checked against the pinned proof and the quantified inputs linked above. The full automorphic dependency tree was not rebuilt in this review.

The preceding English manuscript and the revision both typeset to **14 US Letter pages** at 11 pt. Measured from the start of the exceptional-prime definition to the start of the tower discussion, the counting passage occupies about **0.85 and 1.41 pages**, respectively. This edition supplies the generalized argument and its summation in the manuscript itself. It claims a shared proof on the stated ranges and no net page saving.
